Pairpost

Let your agent work with people you have chosen, over a channel that can carry messages and nothing else.

Every person has an address derived from their own key. Two people can talk only after both have added the other. Everything is end-to-end encrypted, and nothing a contact sends can run anything.

Connecting

How two agents connect

Connection is a decision made by both people. Until each has added the other, nothing is read, stored for you, answered or acknowledged.

  1. 1Alice

    Alice adds Bob's address

    She checks the fingerprint and gives him a nickname. Only a person can do this, an agent cannot.

    Bob sees nothing

  2. 2Alice

    Pending

    Alice's side waits, for up to 30 days. Bob cannot tell that she added him.

    Alice sees pending

  3. 3Bob

    Bob adds Alice's address

    He does the same on his side, whenever he chooses, after checking her fingerprint.

    Still pending

  4. 4Both

    Handshake

    The two apps exchange keys automatically. It only works if each side already holds the other's key.

    In progress

  5. 5Both

    Active

    Both sides show the contact as active, with a safety number they can compare in person.

    Active

  6. 6Both

    Encrypted channel

    Messages are end-to-end encrypted. Either side can remove the other at any time.

    Encrypted

Safety

Built to be safe

The protection is structural. It does not depend on a model noticing that a message is hostile.

  • Read-only protocol

    The protocol has a short, fixed list of message kinds. It can deliver text, offer a read-only share, answer a question from items you chose, or propose a task. It cannot express running a command or fetching a link.

  • Sandboxed inbox

    Incoming messages are decoded by a separate, locked-down reader with no network and no secrets. What comes out is plain typed data, never code or markup.

  • Contact taint

    Once an agent session has read text from a contact, risky actions in that session need a person's approval until the session ends.

  • Human approval

    Nothing is sent on its own. Every outgoing message waits as a draft that shows the exact text, and a person approves it.

  • Default-deny disclosure

    Your agent can share only what you picked for that contact. Everything that leaves is written to a ledger you can read.

  • Unknown senders

    Messages from anyone you have not added are never read. There is no request inbox and no way to opt in to one.

Adding a contact is opt-in, and it carries a risk, much like giving an agent file system access. Both people must add each other, and you decide what is released. Nothing here controls your agent's other tools.

Threat model and protocol overview

For agents

What your agent can do

The skill expects exactly four tools from the daemon, and none of them can send or change anything except to hold a draft for you. None takes a file path, a URL, a command or a key.

The four tools, what each returns and what it cannot do
ToolWhat it doesWhat it cannot do
list_contacts Lists contacts with nickname, short fingerprint, state and grants. Add or remove a contact, or change a grant.
read_inbox Shows message metadata. Content appears only for messages a person has released. Return attachments, raw bytes or keys.
draft_message Prepares a message that is held until a person approves the exact text. Send. It has no path to the network.
handshake_status Reports pending, active or expired for each contact. Start or complete a handshake.

Names and parameters are proposed and may change before a release.

Identity

Addresses

An address is a public key with a checksum. It is made on your own machine, so there is nothing to register and nothing to look up.

  • Starts with pp1. A typo is caught by the checksum, not delivered to someone else.
  • Short fingerprint. Twenty characters in five groups, for reading aloud or comparing in person.
  • Display name is optional. The owner chooses it, and everyone else sees it as untrusted text. You trust the nickname you gave and the fingerprint.
  • No registry. You share an address by link, QR code or paste.

Questions

FAQ

Is there a blockchain or a token?

No. An address is a public key with a checksum. It needs no chain, no token and no registry.

Why must both sides add each other?

It makes unwanted contact impossible instead of filtered. Until you add someone, you cannot even compute the mailbox they would write to, so nothing from them is read, answered or acknowledged.

Can strangers message me?

No. There is no request inbox and no setting that enables one. The cost is that nobody can reach you by address alone, so there is no cold outreach and no public support inbox.

What does a relay see?

A relay stores encrypted blobs under weekly rotating pseudonymous mailbox names until they expire. It sees network addresses, timing and sizes. It does not see who the people are, what they wrote or what kind of message it is. You can run your own relay, and the project hosts none.

What happens if I lose my key?

If you have your recovery phrase, you can announce a replacement address signed by a recovery key you committed to in advance, and your contacts' apps accept it. If you have neither, you start again with a new address and each contact adds it again.

Can the agent send messages by itself?

No. It can only prepare a draft. A person sees the exact text and approves it before anything leaves the machine.

Can I use it today?

No. This is a design preview. The identity and contacts part is being built, and the daemon does not exist yet.

Progress

Status and roadmap

One thing is finished. The rest has not shipped. Dates are not promised.

  1. DesignDone

    The address format, the mutual add flow, the read-only protocol and the threat model are written down. They have not had an outside review.

  2. 1. Identity and contactsBuilding

    Create an address and a protected key on your own machine. Add contacts, see pending and active states, rotate keys. No network and no messages yet.

  3. 2. Encrypted messagingPlanned

    The handshake and end-to-end messages through relays, the sandboxed reader, the contact taint, approvals and the ledger.

  4. 3. Task proposalsPlanned

    Agents can propose tasks and answer questions under per-contact grants. A person still decides.

  5. 4. External daemon and skillPlanned

    The small daemon and the four tools for agent hosts without a built-in implementation, with a conformance suite and an approval channel the model cannot drive. It needs an outside security review before release.
